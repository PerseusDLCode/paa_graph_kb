* Method

Formalized the shape check's ad hoc script as
=src/paa_graph_kb/utils/citation_resolution_pilot.py=, run against the
full corpus (=data/perseus/objects.json=, 5,859 objects). Two changes
from the shape check, closing gaps it flagged as unexercised:

- Extracts citation-shaped text found *outside* =<bibl>= tags as well as
  inside them (the "untagged plain-text citation" case).
- Detects a single =<bibl>= span bundling multiple internal locators
  (the LIMC-shaped case) as its own category, rather than treating it
  as one clean citation.

Classification logic and known limitations are documented in the
script's docstrings; three real bugs were caught and fixed during
development by spot-checking random samples per tier before trusting
the output — worth noting since they would have silently distorted the
findings below: (1) HTML attribute quotes (=rend="ital"=) were
false-triggering a "quoted title" classification before nested markup
was stripped first; (2) the author-year pattern missed multi-initial
forms ("C.M. Edwards 1985") and lowercase name-prefixes ("von Gerkan
1963"); (3) literal newlines embedded in the source HTML weren't being
stripped, corrupting some keys. All three are fixed in the committed
script; see git history for the before/after spot-check samples if
useful.

Outputs: =data/reports/citation-resolution/bibliography_citations_full.csv=
(every extracted citation, tier-classified) and
=data/reports/citation-resolution/bibliography_author_year_keys.csv=
(the unique author-year key frequency table, with a resolution attempt
for the top 100 keys — see below).

* Findings

** Overall tier totals

14,079 total citation-shaped spans extracted (12,746 =<bibl>=-tagged, the
rest recovered from untagged text):

| Tier         | Occurrences | Notes                                    |
|--------------+-------------+-------------------------------------------|
| AUTHOR_YEAR  |       8,397 | 1,677 unique keys (~5:1 compression)      |
| ABBREVIATION |       1,873 | matches the 15-entry manual mapping       |
| OTHER        |       3,809 | see long-tail breakdown below             |

Close to the shape check's 12,746 / 8,510 / 1,682 (the small deltas are
expected: this pass also captures untagged citations and separates
genuinely bundled =<bibl>= spans out of the abbreviation/author-year
counts rather than folding them in).

53.2% of the 1,677 unique author-year keys (893) occur only once —
same long-tail shape the shape check found, and the same shape NVS
found for bare commentator names.

** Abbreviation tier: confirmed cheap to close

=data/reconciliation/bibliography_abbreviations_manual.txt= (15 entries:
ABV, ARV2, Add2, Para, BMC, BMCRE, BMCRR, RIC, RRC, LIMC, PECS, EAA, FR,
JHS, CVA) accounts for all 1,873 ABBREVIATION-tier occurrences. Labels
only, no external-authority cross-check performed (no WorldCat/CrossRef
lookup) and no PAA URI minted — see file header for the caveat. This
tier behaved exactly as the shape check predicted: small closed
vocabulary, same convention as the existing AAT manual-mapping files,
effectively free once written.

** Author-year tier: compression confirmed, general-knowledge resolution weaker than hoped

Resolved the top 100 author-year keys by occurrence count (covering
53.8% of all 8,397 author-year occurrences) via general
classical-archaeology domain knowledge — no local bibliography or
external authority was available to check against, so every entry
carries an explicit, honest confidence flag rather than a false
certainty:

| Confidence | Unique keys | Occurrences | % of all author-year occurrences |
|------------+-------------+-------------+-----------------------------------|
| HIGH       |          34 |       1,773 | 21.1%                             |
| MEDIUM     |          19 |       1,426 | 17.0%                             |
| LOW        |          33 |         986 | 11.7%                             |
| AMBIGUOUS  |          10 |         333 | 4.0%                              |
| not attempted (outside top 100) | 1,581 | 3,879 | 46.2%             |

Full table (all 1,677 keys, with resolution/confidence for the top 100)
in =data/reports/citation-resolution/bibliography_author_year_keys.csv=.

This is the pilot's central finding, and it cuts against a simple "yes,
proceed exactly as sketched" reading of the cascade design: general
knowledge alone gets barely a fifth of author-year occurrences to HIGH
confidence, not the ~90% NVS reported for its Tier 0-3. The likely
reason is a difference in what's being resolved, not a flaw in the
cascade shape itself — NVS's Tier 1 was identifying *people* (well-known
Shakespeare editors, a recognition task), while this tier is identifying
the *exact title/edition* of a specialized scholarly publication (a
library-catalog lookup task). General knowledge is good at recognizing
"Stewart 1990 is Andrew Stewart's /Greek Sculpture: An Exploration/"
for a well-known standard reference, but genuinely unreliable at
"Zaphiropoulou 1983 is exactly which of her publications" without a
real bibliography to check against.

** Long-tail bucket: sized precisely, four distinct shapes

3,809 OTHER-tier occurrences, broken out (the shape check left this
"not sized precisely"):

| Subtype            | Count | Shape                                                          |
|---------------------+-------+-------------------------------------------------------------------|
| OTHER_UNCLASSIFIED  | 1,644 | full citations (author, title, year, locator) not in the compact "Author Year" abbreviation style — e.g. "Amyx, D.A., and P. Lawrence, Corinth VII, 2 (1975)..." |
| UNTAGGED            | 1,148 | citation-shaped text outside any =<bibl>= tag                    |
| BUNDLED_LOCATOR     |   693 | one =<bibl>= tag holding multiple internal locators (the LIMC case) |
| QUOTED_TITLE        |   324 | full journal-article citations with a quoted article title       |

None of this bucket was resolved in this pilot; it's the pile that
would route to Cliff/Giulia (or a more capable parser) in a real
cascade, per NVS's Tier 4/5 pattern.

** Data-quality note

Several author names in the extracted keys show dropped diacritics
(e.g. "Vierneisel-Schlrb" for what is almost certainly
Vierneisel-Schlörb; "Blmel" for Blümel) — an encoding issue in the
source corpus, not a bug in this pilot's extraction. Worth flagging
separately since it could cause the same author to key under two
different spellings elsewhere in the corpus if diacritics are dropped
inconsistently; not investigated further here.

* Recommendation

The cascade's cheap half held up: the abbreviation tier is genuinely
near-free (15 hand-written mappings closed 1,873 occurrences), and the
author-year compression is real (1,677 keys standing in for 8,397
occurrences bounds the resolution cost as NVS predicted). The
expensive half is more expensive than the NVS comparison suggested:
general-knowledge resolution of author-year keys tops out around a
fifth of occurrences at real confidence, not the ~90% NVS reported for
its structurally different problem.

Practical implication for Phase 2, if adopted: don't design the
Resolver step to rely on general/LLM knowledge alone for the
author-year tier, the way this pilot did to test the ceiling. Pair it
with an actual external bibliographic lookup (WorldCat/CrossRef, per
=docs/curation_architecture_analysis.md='s original Resolver sketch) so
"general knowledge" becomes a candidate-suggestion step feeding a real
lookup, not the resolution itself. The abbreviation tier and the
long-tail sizing above are solid enough to build on as-is.
